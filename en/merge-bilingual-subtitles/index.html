How lines are matched

    
Subtitles in different languages rarely split sentences the same way — one Chinese line may be two English cues. Merging by line number quickly drifts out of sync. This tool uses the main subtitle's timing and attaches every second-language line that clearly overlaps it, so small timing differences and split sentences are handled, while neighbouring lines that barely touch are not mixed in.

FAQ

    
Can I merge subtitles with different numbers of lines?
Yes. Lines are matched by overlapping time, not by line number, so it still works when one language splits a sentence into two cues or the timing is slightly off.
Are my files uploaded?
No. Everything runs in your browser and your files never leave your device.
SRT or ASS — which should I choose?
SRT simply stacks the two languages on two lines and works on almost every player and TV. ASS lets the top line be large and white and the bottom line smaller and coloured, which looks nicer, but some TVs and mobile players do not support it.
The two subtitles are out of sync. What should I do?
Align the second subtitle first with a timing tool, then merge. If the subtitles come from different cuts of the video, the gap may not be fully fixable.
