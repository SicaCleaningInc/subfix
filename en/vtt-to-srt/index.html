VTT vs SRT

VTTSRT
HeaderMust start with WEBVTTNone
Cue numbersOptionalRequired
MillisecondsDot: 00:01.000Comma: 00:00:01,000
HoursCan be omittedAlways written
ExtrasPosition, speaker, colour tagsBasic formatting only

What the converter handles

  • Removes the WEBVTT header and NOTE / STYLE blocks.
  • Drops cue settings such as align:start position:10%.
  • Removes speaker <v Name>, colour <c.yellow> and karaoke timestamp tags.
  • Decodes HTML entities like &amp;, adds cue numbers and converts timestamps.

When you need SRT instead of VTT

  • Smart TVs and USB playback: many TVs support SRT but not VTT.
  • Video editors: Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve and CapCut work best with SRT.
  • Older players and devices often only read SRT.

FAQ

Why won't my TV play VTT subtitles?

Many TVs and older players only support SRT. The VTT header, timestamp style and extra tags can confuse them; converting to SRT usually fixes it.

Are cue positions kept?

No. SRT does not support VTT positioning, so subtitles appear in the player's default position, usually at the bottom.

Are my files uploaded?

No. Everything runs in your browser and your files never leave your device.

Is the WebVTT to SRT converter free?

Yes. It is free with no sign-up and no file limit, and it runs entirely in your browser.

Can I just rename .vtt to .srt?

Not reliably. VTT has a WEBVTT header and uses a dot before milliseconds instead of a comma, so many players reject a renamed file.

Will the timing change after converting?

No. The timecodes stay the same; only the format of the timestamps changes.
