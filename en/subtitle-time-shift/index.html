How to use

  1. Measure the gap: find a clear line of dialogue and compare when the actor speaks with when the subtitle appears.
  2. Enter the offset: positive to delay, negative to advance. The preview shows timestamps before and after.
  3. Download: the file keeps its name, so just replace the old one. A whole season can be adjusted at once and downloaded as a ZIP.

Two kinds of out-of-sync subtitles

1. The whole file is off by the same amount

This is the most common case. The subtitle was made for a different release of the video — one with a longer or shorter intro, an extra studio logo, or ads. Because the gap is constant, a single offset fixes it.

2. In sync at the start, worse towards the end

This is almost always a frame-rate mismatch. Films are shot at 24 (or 23.976) frames per second, but European PAL releases are sped up to 25 fps, making the film about 4% shorter — roughly five minutes over a two-hour movie. A subtitle timed for the 25 fps release will drift earlier and earlier on a 23.976 fps video, and vice versa.

Quick fix in VLC

For a one-off viewing, press G or H in VLC to move subtitles by 50 ms per press. The setting is usually not saved, so fixing the file is better if you will watch again or share it.

How to fix subtitle delay

Subtitle delay is the gap between when a line is spoken and when it appears. Fix it in three steps:

  1. Find a clear line near the start of the video and note the time the actor starts speaking, e.g. 00:05:12.5.
  2. Find the same line in the subtitle (or watch when it appears), e.g. 00:05:10.5.
  3. Enter the difference in the tool above. Here the subtitle is 2 seconds early, so enter 2 to delay it. If it were 2 seconds late, enter -2.

Then check a line near the end of the video. If it is still in sync, you are done. If the gap has grown, you also need a frame-rate change.

How to change subtitle frame rate (23.976, 24, 25 fps)

If subtitles start in sync but slowly drift, the subtitle was timed for a video with a different frame rate. Choose the matching conversion in the tool:

FAQ

Subtitles appear before the dialogue — positive or negative?

If subtitles show up too early, they need to be delayed: enter a positive number. If they appear too late, enter a negative number to bring them earlier.

Subtitles drift further out of sync over time. What now?

If the start is in sync but the gap grows towards the end, the subtitle and video have different frame rates — for example a 25 fps subtitle on a 23.976 fps video. Use the frame-rate conversion option, then fine-tune with the offset.

Are my files uploaded?

No. Everything runs in your browser and your files never leave your device.

Which formats are supported?

SRT, VTT, ASS, SSA, SBV, LRC and SMI. Frame-based MicroDVD SUB files are not supported.

How do I know if subtitles are early or late?

If you read the line before you hear it, the subtitle is early: enter a positive number to delay it. If you hear the line first, the subtitle is late: enter a negative number.

Can I sync subtitles online without installing anything?

Yes. This tool runs entirely in your browser on Windows, Mac, Linux, iPhone and Android. Nothing is installed and your files are not uploaded.

Can I shift only part of a subtitle file?

The tool shifts the whole file. If only the second half is off (for example after an extra scene), split the file, shift the second part and join them, or look for a subtitle made for your version.

Does shifting change the subtitle text or formatting?

No. Only the timecodes change. Text, line breaks and ASS styling stay exactly the same.
