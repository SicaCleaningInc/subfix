Fix Garbled Chinese Subtitles When Uploading SRT to YouTube

Updated: 2026-11-18

Subtitles increase watch time and open your videos to more viewers. But Chinese SRT files sometimes turn into gibberish after upload, or the timing is off. Here is how to fix both.

Quick fix: Auto-detects GBK, Big5, Shift-JIS and EUC-KR and converts to UTF-8. Files never leave your device.
Open Garbled Subtitle Fixer

Garbled Chinese: the file is not UTF-8

YouTube recommends UTF-8 for subtitle files. Subtitles from older software or downloaded online are often GBK or Big5, which show up garbled.

Before uploading, convert the file with the Garbled Subtitle Fixer, check the preview, then upload the downloaded file.

Uploading subtitles in YouTube Studio

  1. Open YouTube Studio and choose Subtitles in the left menu.
  2. Pick the video and add a language, e.g. Chinese (Hong Kong) or Chinese (Taiwan).
  3. Choose Upload file → With timing, then select the SRT file.
  4. Check the preview and publish.

YouTube updates its interface regularly, so button names may differ slightly.

Timing is off

If you trimmed the start of the video, every line will be early or late. Shift them all at once with the Subtitle Time Shift tool and upload again.

Offer both Simplified and Traditional

Create the second version with the Simplified to Traditional or Traditional to Simplified converter, and upload them as separate languages.
