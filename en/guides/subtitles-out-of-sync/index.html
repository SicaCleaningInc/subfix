Subtitles Out of Sync: 3 Causes and How to Fix Each

Updated: 2026-11-20

Subtitles that are too early or too late have three main causes. Each needs a different fix, so diagnose first.

Quick fix: Shift a whole subtitle file earlier or later, or convert between frame rates.

Step 1: Work out which kind of drift you have

Pick one line near the start, one in the middle and one near the end, and compare when the subtitle appears with when the line is spoken.

What you seeCauseFix
Same offset everywhereDifferent intro lengthShift everything
Fine at the start, worse laterDifferent frame rateFrame-rate conversion
Suddenly off after one pointDifferent cut of the videoShift in parts, or find another subtitle
B

Cause 1: Different intro length

The same film or episode can come in versions with or without studio logos, recaps or ads. A subtitle made for another version is off by a fixed number of seconds.

Measure it: note when a character starts speaking (e.g. 00:05:12.5) and when the subtitle appears (e.g. 00:05:10.5). The subtitle is 2 seconds early, so delay it by 2 seconds.

Fix: enter 2 in the Subtitle Time Shift tool and download. Use a negative number if subtitles are late.

C

Cause 2: Different frame rate

Films are usually shot at 24 (or 23.976) frames per second. PAL releases play at 25 fps, about 4% faster, so a two-hour film runs roughly 5 minutes shorter.

A subtitle made for a 25 fps release on a 23.976 fps video starts almost in sync, then appears earlier and earlier, minutes off by the end. The reverse makes it later and later.

  • Subtitles appear earlier and earlier: convert 25 fps → 23.976 fps.
  • Subtitles appear later and later: convert 23.976 fps → 25 fps.

Use the frame-rate option in the Subtitle Time Shift tool, then fine-tune any remaining offset. To check a video's frame rate in VLC: Tools → Media Information → Codec.

D

Cause 3: A different cut

If the subtitles are fine until a certain point and then jump out of sync, the video probably has an extra or removed scene (director's cut, TV edit). A single shift or frame-rate conversion can't fix that. The practical fix is to find a subtitle that matches your version; if there's only one jump, split the file, shift the second half, and join them.

E

Watching just once? Adjust in the player

Most desktop players can delay subtitles on the fly. In VLC, press G or H to move them by 50 ms. The change usually isn't saved, so edit the file if you'll watch again or share it. You can also test timing in the online subtitle player and download the corrected SRT.

FAQ

How do I know if subtitles are early or late?

If you read the line before you hear it, the subtitle is early: delay it (positive number). If you hear it first, the subtitle is late: advance it (negative number).

What frame rate is my video?

In VLC, open Tools → Media Information → Codec. On Windows, the file's Properties → Details also show the frame rate.

Does shifting change the subtitle text?

No. Only the timecodes change; text, formatting and line numbers stay the same.
