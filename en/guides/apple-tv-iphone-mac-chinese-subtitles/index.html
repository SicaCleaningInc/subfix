Chinese Subtitles Showing Boxes on Apple TV, iPhone and Mac

Updated: 2026-11-16

If your Chinese subtitles suddenly turned into rows of empty boxes (□□□) after updating your Apple TV, iPhone, iPad or Mac, you are not alone. Users of Infuse, Plex and VLC reported the same problem after Apple's 2024 system updates. It is a font problem, not a broken file — and there are several ways around it.

What happened

From iOS 18, tvOS 18 and macOS 15, Chinese text drawn by some video apps started appearing as boxes, mostly with ASS subtitles. Reports on the Infuse community forum noted that SRT subtitles were not affected — only ASS. Developers on Apple's forums linked related problems to changes in how macOS 15 handles the PingFang system font.

Boxes or gibberish? Check first

  • Empty boxes □□□ → a font problem. Use the fixes below.

Fix 1: Update your player app

App developers shipped fixes after the system updates. For example, Infuse 7.8.4 included fixes for subtitles on iOS/tvOS 18 and macOS 15. Make sure Infuse, Plex or VLC is on the latest version before trying anything else.

Fix 2: Convert ASS to SRT

Because the problem mainly affects ASS subtitles, converting to SRT is the quickest universal workaround. Use the ASS to SRT converter — it removes styling and effects but keeps the dialogue and timing, and the app then draws the text with its own fonts.

Fix 3: Plex on Apple TV — try the old video player

Plex users on Apple TV reported that turning on the "use old video player" option in Plex's settings made Chinese subtitles display correctly again, at the cost of some subtitle styling.

Fix 4: Install the Chinese system font on iPhone and iPad

Some users found that newer iOS and iPadOS versions no longer had the Simplified Chinese PingFang font installed by default, and that downloading it under Settings → General → Fonts fixed Chinese ASS subtitles. Menu names may vary between versions.

Still not working?

  • Check whether the same subtitle works in another app. If it does, report the problem to the app developer with a sample file.
  • If you only need Chinese and English together, merge them into one SRT — SRT is the most widely supported format.

The workarounds above are based on user and developer reports on the Infuse, Plex and Apple developer forums. Apple and app developers may change behaviour in later updates.
