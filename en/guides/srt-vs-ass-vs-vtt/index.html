SRT vs ASS vs VTT: Subtitle Formats Explained

Updated: 2026-11-20

SRT, ASS/SSA and WebVTT all do the same basic job: show text at the right time. They differ in styling, precision and how widely they are supported.

A

SRT: simplest and most compatible

SRT (SubRip) is the most widespread format. Each cue has a number, a timecode and the text, separated by blank lines.

1
00:00:01,000 --> 00:00:03,500
Hello, and welcome.

2
00:00:04,000 --> 00:00:06,000
It's a lovely day.

Timecodes are hours:minutes:seconds,milliseconds, with a comma before the milliseconds. SRT is text only; some players support simple tags like <i>, but not position, colour or effects.

Best for: everyday viewing, TVs and set-top boxes, uploading to most platforms.

B

ASS / SSA: styled subtitles with effects

ASS (Advanced SubStation Alpha) and its predecessor SSA are popular for anime fansubs. Besides dialogue, the file holds styles for font, size, colour, position and outline, and can do karaoke and animated effects.

[V4+ Styles]
Style: Default,Arial,20,&H00FFFFFF,...

[Events]
Dialogue: 0,0:00:01.00,0:00:03.50,Default,,0,0,0,,{\an8}Hello, and welcome.

Timing is in hundredths of a second. {\an8} is an override tag that moves the line to the top of the screen.

Best for: computer playback and styled subtitles. Many TVs and phone players don't support it or ignore the styling; convert with ASS to SRT when needed.

C

VTT: the web video standard

WebVTT is the subtitle format for HTML5 video, used by YouTube and many course platforms. It looks like SRT but starts with WEBVTT, uses a dot before milliseconds, and the hours can be omitted.

WEBVTT

00:01.000 --> 00:03.500 line:90%
Hello, and welcome.

Settings such as line:90% after the timecode control position.

Best for: web video and online courses. Some TVs and older players don't support it; use VTT to SRT, or SRT to TXT for plain text. To watch a local video with a VTT file, try the online subtitle player.

The problem formats don't solve: encoding

Whatever the format, the most common problem with Chinese subtitles is encoding. The same SRT saved as GBK or Big5 shows up garbled in many players. Stick to UTF-8; the Garbled Subtitle Fixer converts in one click.

FAQ

Which subtitle format should I use?

SRT, unless you need styling (ASS) or are publishing web video (VTT). SRT works almost everywhere.

Will I lose anything converting ASS to SRT?

Yes: fonts, colours, positions and effects are dropped. The text and timing stay.

Can I rename .vtt to .srt?

Not reliably. The header and the dot before milliseconds differ, so some players reject it. Convert it properly instead.
