Fix Chinese Subtitles on Samsung and LG TVs

Updated: 2026-11-20

Samsung and LG TVs can play videos with external subtitles from USB, but they are far pickier than a computer. Check these in order.

1. Garbled subtitles

Some Samsung and LG models have an encoding option in the subtitle settings during playback, where you can try a Chinese encoding. Options vary a lot between models and software versions, and some models have none.

Most reliable fix: convert the file with the Garbled Subtitle Fixer and keep "add BOM" on. UTF-8 with BOM is the format TVs recognise most reliably.

B

2. Subtitles not showing

  • Use exactly the video's file name, in the same USB folder. See subtitle file naming.
  • Open the playback menu and check subtitles are turned on.
  • Use SRT. ASS may show as plain text or not at all on some models; convert to SRT.
  • Embedded MKV subtitles missing? Extract them to SRT and use them as external subtitles.
C

3. Boxes instead of characters

With the TV language set to English, some models may not display Chinese subtitles properly. Try switching the TV language to Chinese.

D

4. Subtitles out of sync

Fix the file with the Subtitle Time Shift tool instead of adjusting on the TV every time.

E

Still not working?

Stream to the TV with Plex or Jellyfin, which handle subtitles much better. See Plex, Jellyfin & Emby Chinese subtitles.

FAQ

Which subtitle formats do Samsung and LG TVs support?

SRT is the safest. Support for SMI, SUB, ASS and others depends on the model; check your TV's manual.

Can I show two subtitles at once on the TV?

Usually not. Merge both languages into one SRT with the bilingual subtitle merger.

Does the USB drive format matter?

The TV must be able to read the drive (FAT32 or exFAT are the most compatible), but subtitle problems are almost always about the subtitle file itself.
