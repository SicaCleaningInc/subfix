Fix Chinese Subtitles on Xiaomi TV, Android TV & Google TV

Updated: 2026-11-18

Xiaomi TVs and boxes, TCL, Sony and most Google TV devices run Android. Their built-in video players handle subtitles far worse than a computer, and the usual problems are garbled text, boxes, or no subtitles at all.

Problem 1: Garbled subtitles

Built-in players mostly expect UTF-8. Older GBK or Big5 subtitles that look fine on a PC turn into gibberish on the TV.

Fix: convert the file with the Garbled Subtitle Fixer and keep the "add BOM" option on; some TVs need the BOM to recognise UTF-8.

B

Problem 2: Subtitles not showing

  • Name mismatch: the subtitle must match the video, e.g. Movie.mp4 and Movie.srt.
  • Unsupported format: built-in players have limited ASS/SSA support. Convert to SRT.
  • Different folder: keep the subtitle in the same folder on the USB drive or NAS.
C

Problem 3: Boxes instead of characters

With the system language set to English, some built-in players do not load a Chinese font. Try switching the TV language to Chinese, or use one of the players below.

D

Best fix: install a third-party player

Install VLC, Kodi or MX Player from Google Play. Their subtitle format, encoding and font support is far more complete.

  • VLC: free, no ads, lets you set the subtitle encoding.
  • Kodi: great for large libraries; see the Kodi Chinese subtitles guide.
  • Streaming from a NAS? See Plex, Jellyfin & Emby Chinese subtitles.

Want Chinese and English at the same time?

FAQ

Which player is best for subtitles on Android TV?

VLC and Kodi are free and handle encodings, formats and fonts well. Kodi is better for large libraries.

Why does my Xiaomi TV show subtitles for some videos but not others?

Usually the file name doesn't exactly match the video, or the subtitle is ASS. Rename it to match and convert to SRT.

Can I use a USB drive formatted as NTFS?

Many Android TVs read NTFS, but FAT32 and exFAT are the most compatible. If the TV doesn't see your files, try exFAT.

Do embedded MKV subtitles work on Android TV?

Often yes, but not always. If not, extract them to SRT.
